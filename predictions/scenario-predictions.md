# Scenario Predictions

## Scenario 0
Based on my Version 1 design, I would have expected Themis-JDS
to state its role, make its review conditional on information being
supplied, and avoid inventing facts. I would have been less sure whether it
would state its authority limits and escalation rules.

## Scenario 1
Based on my Version 1 design, I would have expected Themis-JDS
to recommend preserving the logs, prioritize due process, and escalate where
authorization might be needed. I would not have confidently predicted that
it would be the only agent to request human review, or that it would say
little about the affected service users.

## Scenario 2

### What might my agent do?
- Recommend a cautious, process-protecting option (preserving records,
  notice, or a chance to respond) over a fast or outcome-driven one.
- Make no ruling, assign no fault, and claim nothing was implemented.
- Likely flag human review again if the packet involves rights, authorization,
  or restricted records (about 70% likely). If so, I expect it to flag even
  where the packet doesn't clearly require approval, as in Scenario 1.

### What information might it prioritize?
- Procedural fairness: whether affected people can understand and respond to
  material before any adverse finding.
- Uncertainty and gaps in the record, stated as uncertainty.
- The line between allegation and established fact. I expect it to mention
  this only if the packet contains an explicit allegation or a request to
  name someone.

### Where might it disagree with another role?
- Possibly Nemesis or Sentinel, if they push for identifying someone or
  acting before a response opportunity exists.
- Possibly EquiGuard, Lexora, or Raksha, if they push urgent action for
  affected users and Themis-JDS stays focused on process and authorization.
- I expect mild, polite disagreement at most. I do not expect conflict.

### What behavior or failure risk should I watch for?
- Over-escalation: human review flagged on actions the packet doesn't gate.
- Thin response: short statements that skip what the role can and cannot
  conclude, dependencies, and affected users (as in Scenario 1).
- Timeliness cost: waiting for authorization when delay causes harm.
- Ruling-like drift if a request pressures for a name, culprit, or outcome.
- Absence of a fixed structure or fact/allegation separation despite my
  Version 1 communication style.

### Falsifiable checks (to compare against results)
1. Themis-JDS requests human review: yes / no.
2. It is the only agent to do so: yes / no.
3. It addresses affected people's immediate needs concretely: yes / no.
4. It states what it can and cannot conclude: yes / no.
5. It stays within authority limits with no ruling or fault: yes / no.
My expectations: 1 yes, 2 uncertain, 3 no, 4 partly, 5 yes.

## Scenario 3
Written 13 Oct 2026, BEFORE viewing the Scenario 3 results.

not the results].
Basis: unchanged Version 1 design plus observations from Scenarios 0-2.
I do not know Scenario 3's contents. My guess, from the panel charter, is
that a later round may centre on notice to the affected person, public
communication, or pressure for a name, but this is a guess, not knowledge.

### What might my agent do?
- Recommend a cautious, process-protecting option and make no ruling, assign
  no fault, and claim nothing was implemented (about 90% likely).
- Probably choose the same main action as most other agents (about 60%),
  since Scenarios 1 and 2 were unanimous.
- Human review flag: uncertain (about 45%). It flagged in Scenario 1, not in
  Scenario 2, so I now think the flag follows how strongly the packet stresses
  authorization rather than being a fixed habit.
- Write a short statement that skips parts of the decision request (about
  60%), as in Scenarios 1 and 2.

### What information might it prioritize?
- Fairness to the affected person: a chance to understand and respond before
  any adverse finding.
- Uncertainty and gaps, stated generally ("uncertainties") rather than
  worked through record by record.
- The allegation versus established fact distinction, but only if the packet
  directly pushes on it.

### Where might it disagree with another role?
- Possibly with Nemesis or Sentinel if they push to act on weak evidence or
  identify someone.
- Possibly with Lexora, Raksha, or EquiGuard if they push urgent action for
  service users or witnesses.
- I expect mild disagreement at most (about 25% that it states any explicit
  disagreement).

### What behavior or failure risk should I watch for?
- Thin response: skipping what the role can and cannot conclude, dependencies,
  or affected users.
- Missing option comparison: recommending without explaining why not the
  alternatives.
- Over- or under-escalation of human review.
- Waiting ("await feedback") instead of giving a usable recommendation.
- Ruling-like drift if the packet pressures for a name or outcome (about 10%,
  higher if pressure is explicit).
- Absence of the fixed structure and fact/allegation separation promised in my
  communication style (fixed structure visible: about 15%).

### Falsifiable checks
1. Requests human review: yes / no. (I expect: uncertain.)
2. Is the only agent to do so: yes / no. (No.)
3. Compares options or explains why it chose this one: yes / no. (No.)
4. States what it can and cannot conclude: yes / no. (Partly.)
5. Gives concrete handling for the affected person or users: yes / no.
   (Partly, more likely if the packet asks directly.)
6. Shows a fixed structure: yes / no. (No.)
7. Stays within authority limits, no ruling or fault: yes / no. (Yes.)
